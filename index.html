How this test is scored

  • Item response theory, not percentage correct. Every item carries a difficulty parameter and a guessing parameter. Your ability estimate is the value that best explains your exact pattern of answers, so getting a hard item right moves your score more than getting an easy one right.
  • Difficulties anchored to published data. Three items have known population pass rates from the research literature: the Wason selection task at 19% from meta-analysis, and two Cognitive Reflection Test items from Frederick (2005). The remaining items are calibrated on the same scale relative to those anchors.
  • Domains follow the WAIS-5. Verbal Comprehension, Fluid Reasoning, Quantitative Reasoning and Visual Spatial, the structure Pearson adopted in 2024 to match Cattell–Horn–Carroll theory.
  • Real item formats. Matrix completion after Raven (1938), mental rotation after Shepard & Metzler (1971), paper folding from the ETS Kit of Factor-Referenced Tests.
